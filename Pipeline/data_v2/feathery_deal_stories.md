# Feathery Deal Stories

Customer names are kept confidential, so the stories below describe customers by type.

## Story 1: Running full deal cycles as the AE (two midsize brokers)

**Situation:** Feathery is a startup and AE bandwidth is limited. Some prospects Vaughn met came from his own business development, especially at insurance industry conferences such as **Guidewire Connections** and **NAMIC**, where he has the **highest meetings-booked-per-conference rate among the SEs** (about **7 meetings** from a good conference).

**What he did:** For **two midsize insurance brokers**, Vaughn stepped into the **Account Executive role** and ran the whole deal cycle. Part of it was necessity: he sourced the prospects, and the AEs were at capacity. Part of it was his own choice: he asked to lead the cycles to build full-cycle sales experience. One deal centered on Feathery's **Employee Benefits tool** and the other on the **quote comparison tool**. He owned every stage:

- First outreach and initial conversations
- **MEDDPICC-driven discovery**: pain, metrics, economic buyer, decision process, champion
- Designing and building the custom demo himself
- Moving the deals into **contract negotiation**, where they are now

**Why it matters:** It shows Vaughn can generate his own pipeline, qualify it rigorously, run the technical and commercial sides of a deal at the same time, and move it toward close.

## Story 2: Selling ahead of the roadmap

**Situation:** At a startup, prospects regularly come in looking for a capability that isn't fully built yet. Twice this happened with workflows the market was actively asking for: a **certificate of insurance (COI) workflow** and an **Employee Benefits workflow**.

**What he did:** Vaughn didn't just tell prospects it was "on the roadmap." He **fully built out demos** of both workflows on Feathery's platform, so the prospects could see exactly how the solution would work for their business. He has built custom demos like these **from scratch in under a week** several times.

**Result:** The prospects stayed engaged, and every one of those deals is still active and on a good path.

## Story 3: Saving an at-risk customer after a platform regression

**Situation:** A Feathery **platform update caused accuracy regressions** in a tool an existing customer relied on. The customer was understandably frustrated, and the account was at risk. Vaughn was brought in to support the customer through the recovery.

**What he did:**

- **Owned it.** Feathery was open about the regressions being its fault. There was no deflecting or minimizing.
- **Responded fast and fixed fast.** The team was quick to respond and resolved the issues in a timely way.
- **Made the customer feel heard.** Much of the work was people management: regular conversations with the customer, making sure their concerns were heard, acknowledging that they were right and that Feathery had made a mistake.
- **Showed why it wouldn't happen again.** Beyond the fix, Feathery **put systems in place to prevent this kind of regression from recurring**, and Vaughn walked the customer through those safeguards so they could trust the platform going forward.

**Result:** **The customer stayed.** Vaughn credits that to the support Feathery showed, the speed of the response, honesty about what went wrong, and visible proof that it had been fixed at the root.

**What it shows:** Vaughn can manage a tense customer relationship with empathy and accountability. He knows that how a vendor handles a mistake often matters more to trust than the mistake itself. It echoes the on-call and incident-response discipline he built as an engineer at DraftKings: mitigate quickly, communicate clearly, and fix the root cause.
